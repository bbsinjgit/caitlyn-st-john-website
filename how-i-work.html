How I work

I practise relational-developmental integrative psychotherapy. That means drawing on what we know about human development, neuroscience and relationships to understand your story, your difficulties and what you need.

The space I provide is collaborative. You are the expert on what it is like to be you. I bring my training and experience, and together we work out what you need. I stay present and alongside you while you determine how we use our time. I’m warm, authentic and curious.

Therapy isn’t only about talking. We pay attention to your thoughts, your emotions and what your body is telling you, and to the patterns that show up in how you relate to others. Often the most important things are the ones we haven’t yet put into words.

Because so much of our pain comes from relationships, the relationship between us becomes part of the work. It can be a place to feel safe, understood and taken seriously, perhaps in a way you haven’t before. That experience can slowly change how you feel in your other relationships, and with yourself.

I draw upon attachment theory, Gestalt, relational and psychoanalytic theories, Internal Family Systems, developmental psychology, and neuroscience.

Relational

Our relationship is at the heart of the work, a safe place to notice and try new ways of connecting.

Attachment-based

Exploring how early bonds shaped the way you relate to yourself and others now.

Integrative

Drawing on different ideas and methods to fit you, rather than fitting you to a method.

What I can help with

People often come to me feeling stuck, overwhelmed or alone. They bring anxiety, depression, trauma, grief and loss, difficult family relationships, questions of identity, strong self-criticism, and questions of faith and meaning.

I welcome any difficulties you are experiencing. In addition to my developmental-relational psychotherapy training, and my experience working in long-term and short-term therapy, I also bring personal and professional understanding of life between cultures, cross-cultural relationships and identity, supporting loved ones through mental health struggles, and growing up with religion.

Depression

When life feels flat, heavy or hopeless, and it is hard to find a way forward.

Anxiety

Worry, overwhelm and a body that will not settle, and what may lie underneath.

Relational difficulties

Painful patterns that make closeness, trust or belonging feel hard.
