A place to be met in your pain, and seen.

People come to therapy for all sorts of reasons. Some are living with depression or anxiety. Some are grieving. Some want to understand themselves better, or feel stuck in ways they can’t quite shift. Others are struggling in their relationships, or living with the aftermath of trauma.

Whatever brings you, therapy can be a place to engage with your story, understand your patterns of relating, and be accompanied through it rather than left to work it out alone.

We all feel lonely, frustrated, exhausted, curious, anxious, confused, misunderstood and angry at times. I would love to meet you just as you are.

“Good mental health starts with kindness towards yourself.”

Try noticing how you speak to yourself after you’ve got something wrong, or after a stressful day. Are there patterns? Is there a kinder, more understanding response you could offer?

How I work

I practise relational-developmental integrative psychotherapy. That means drawing on what we know about human development, neuroscience and relationships to understand your story, your difficulties and what you need.

In each session I aim to meet you wherever you are that day, and to help you meet yourself: your thoughts, feelings, body, behaviour and relationships. The relationship we build becomes a place to learn to relate more deeply to yourself and others, and to notice how the past may be shaping the present. Always, we work toward your own goals.

Relational

Our relationship is at the heart of the work, a safe place to notice and try new ways of connecting.

Attachment-based

Exploring how early bonds shaped the way you relate to yourself and others now.

Humanistic

Trusting your capacity for growth, and meeting you with warmth rather than judgement.

Integrative

Drawing on different ideas and methods to fit you, rather than fitting you to a method.

Therapy can be found in many spaces.

As well as meeting online, you can choose to have your sessions in a cosy canvas bell tent, surrounded by the fields, woodland and birdsong of East Devon. You’re sheltered and comfortable inside, with the natural world all around you.

For some people, stepping out of a clinical room and into a quieter, greener space makes it easier to slow down, notice what they feel, and talk. The setting doesn’t replace the therapy. It is there to support it.

Caitlyn St John

Integrative psychotherapist, MSc, MUKCP

I’m originally from California and have made England my home for many years. I grew up close to someone working in psychotherapy, and have always been fascinated by how our earliest relationships shape who we are today.

Being invited into someone’s story, and walking alongside them through its ups and downs, feels like a real privilege. My experience includes time-limited therapy with students at the University of Leicester and longer-term work in private practice.

Outside the therapy room you’ll find me in the mountains or the woods, cooking, playing sport with my family, or over coffee with friends.
